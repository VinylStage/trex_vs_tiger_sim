What is the T-Rex vs Tiger Simulator?

What happens if N Tyrannosaurus rex meet M tigers on an open field? This free educational tool uses that question to let you try the Monte Carlo method yourself, and it also offers match-ups observed in the wild, such as elephants and lions or brown bears and wolves. Instead of a single fight, it repeats the battle hundreds of times with uncertain, literature-based traits and shows the win probabilities together with their uncertainty.

How does it work?

  1. Choose the species and head counts on both sides, the pack tactic and the number of runs, and optionally edit the ranges of the 22 traits.
  2. Every battle draws fresh traits, such as body mass, bite force and speed, from those ranges.
  3. Your browser computes many agent-based battles, in which each animal follows simple rules.
  4. The results show win probabilities with 95% confidence intervals, battle durations, a sensitivity chart and a replay.

What can you learn?

  • Monte Carlo simulation: approximating an answer by repeating random experiments
  • Confidence intervals: how the estimate sharpens as the number of runs grows
  • Sensitivity analysis: which assumptions the outcome depends on most

Frequently asked questions

Did Tyrannosaurus rex and tigers ever meet?

No. Tyrannosaurus rex lived at the end of the Cretaceous, about 68 to 66 million years ago, and tigers appeared much later. The match-up is a hypothetical scenario for learning the method.

So who wins?

There is no fixed answer. The win probability depends strongly on assumptions such as head counts, morale, tactics and trait ranges. Preliminary results for the default assumptions, and how to read them, are on the model page, and you can check them yourself by opening the simulator.

What is a Monte Carlo simulation?

It repeats the same experiment many times with uncertain values drawn at random and estimates the answer from the spread of the results. The more runs, the narrower the confidence interval of the win probability.

Can I share or reproduce a result?

Yes. Copy link creates an address that holds the species, head counts, parameter ranges and seed. Opening that link and running it gives the same result.

Which animals can I choose?

The large animals are Tyrannosaurus rex, the African bush elephant, the African buffalo, the brown bear, the woolly mammoth and Homo erectus; the pack animals are the tiger, the lion, the gray wolf, the spotted hyena and Homo erectus. Match-ups observed in the wild, such as elephants and lions, buffalo and lions or brown bears and wolves, are marked, and every preset value shows its evidence level (literature, derived, speculative or unverified). Homo erectus, an extinct human species, is a thought experiment on physical capability alone, without clothing, tools or weapons; modern humans and domestic animals are not included.
