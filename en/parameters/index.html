Parameter reference: 22 model parameters and the scenario settings

Every value you can change in the simulator. For each parameter: the default range and allowed bounds, the unit, whether it varies between animals, what it does in the engine, and where the range comes from.

How to read this page

  • Default range (min · mode · max): the triangular distribution each run draws from. Values near the mode are drawn most often, values near min and max more rarely. You can change it under the simulator's advanced settings.
  • Allowed bounds: typed values and values from shared links are clamped to this range. Values multiplied by individual variation never leave it either.
  • Individual variation: “Yes” means animals within the same run differ a little (the size is set by Individual variation (CV)). “No” means every animal in the run shares the value.
  • Literature-based / Speculative: literature-based ranges rest on published measurements or models. Speculative ranges have no direct data and are deliberately wide judgement calls; treat results that depend on them with extra care.
  • Units: s is seconds, 1/s a per-second rate, HP the health unit (1 kg = 1 HP). Probabilities and morale are values between 0 and 1.

How the values interact in a battle is explained in How the model works; a sequence of experiments to try is in the tutorial of the guide.

Large-animal parameters (11)

Body, movement, attack and shake-off values of the large animal that fights alone. Large animals have no stamina and no morale. The ranges and sources below are for the default species, an adult Tyrannosaurus rex; the other species are under Species presets. For the elephant, buffalo and brown bear the bite names read as attacks (tusks, horns, paws); the arithmetic is the same.

  • Body mass
  • Speed
  • Turn rate
  • Bite force
  • Bite lethality
  • Bite interval
  • Bite reach
  • Bite arc (half-angle)
  • Bite accuracy
  • Shake-off rate
  • Body radius

Body mass heavy.mass Literature-based

Default range (min · mode · max)
5,650 · 8,000 · 9,500 kg
Allowed bounds
1,000 – 20,000 kg
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Sets the T-Rex maximum HP (1 kg = 1 HP). It is also the denominator of the carried-mass slowdown, so a heavier T-Rex is slowed less by latched tigers.

Basis. The minimum is close to the specimen “Sue”, about 5,654 kg [Erickson 2004]; “Scotty” is about 8,870 kg [Persons 2019]; volumetric models of large adults exceed 9 t [Hutchinson 2011]. The mode of 8 t is a central adult value.

Speed heavy.speed Literature-based

Default range (min · mode · max)
3.4 · 5.5 · 8 m/s
Allowed bounds
0.5 – 25 m/s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Speed while chasing a target. The T-Rex only advances while the target is farther than 80% of its bite reach; latched tigers slow it by their weight, and speed halves while its heading is more than 60° off the target.

Basis. Fast running is ruled out [Hutchinson & Garcia 2002], and stress-constrained models allow only walking gaits up to about 5 m/s [Sellers 2017], which motivates the mode. The minimum is a brisk walk above the preferred 1.28 m/s [van Bijlert 2021]; the 8 m/s maximum leaves room for less conservative estimates. The exact numbers are judgement.

Turn rate heavy.turnRate Literature-based

Default range (min · mode · max)
22 · 30 · 45 °/s
Allowed bounds
5 – 360 °/s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. The heading can change by at most “turn rate × 0.1” every 0.1 s. The slower the turn, the harder it is to keep tigers that circle to the side or rear in front of the jaws.

Basis. Large rotational inertia implies about 1–2 s per 45° turn [Hutchinson, Ng-Thow-Hing & Anderson 2007], i.e. 22.5–45 °/s.

Bite force heavy.biteForce Literature-based

Default range (min · mode · max)
8.5 · 35 · 57 kN
Allowed bounds
1 – 150 kN
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. A bite that connects deals “bite force × bite lethality × U(0.7, 1.3)”. It largely decides whether one bite kills a tiger.

Basis. Estimates are 8,526–34,522 N [Gignac & Erickson 2017] and a maximum of 35–57 kN [Bates & Falkingham 2012]. The minimum and mode follow the first study, the maximum the second.

Bite lethality heavy.biteLethality Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
4 · 8 · 12 HP/kN
Allowed bounds
0.1 – 100 HP/kN
Individual variation
No: set per run only

What it does. Converts bite force into damage: HP per kN. At the default modes one bite deals 196–364 HP, which usually kills a 180 HP tiger at once.

Basis. No data maps bite force to damage. The range deliberately spans “one bite kills” (upper end) to “one bite only wounds” (lower end: 8.5 kN × 4 HP/kN = 24–44 HP per hit).

Bite interval heavy.biteInterval Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
1.5 · 2.5 · 4 s
Allowed bounds
0.2 – 30 s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Time between one bite attempt and the next. The first wait at the start of a battle is random between 0 and this value.

Basis. A judgement combining the jaw cycle and the time to re-aim the head.

Bite reach heavy.biteReach Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
4.5 · 5.5 · 6.5 m
Allowed bounds
1 – 15 m
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. How far from its centre the T-Rex can bite; the hit test adds the tiger radius of 0.8 m. The T-Rex stops advancing once its target is within 80% of this distance, and it also sets the flanking tigers’ danger zone (reach + 4 m).

Basis. The skull is about 1.5 m long [Padian 2022; Carpenter & Smith 2001]. With a 2.5 m body radius, the default puts the jaws about 3 m beyond the body circle, covering skull and neck. A geometric estimate.

Bite arc (half-angle) heavy.biteArc Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
30 · 45 · 70 °
Allowed bounds
5 – 180 °
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Half-angle either side of straight ahead in which the T-Rex can bite; the full cone is twice as wide. Flanking tigers try to stay outside “bite arc + 20°”.

Basis. The bite is the only modelled weapon. Fossil evidence of tyrannosaur attacks is bite evidence: a healed bite on a hadrosaur [DePalma 2013] and face-biting between tyrannosaurs [Peterson 2009]. The angle itself is judgement.

Bite accuracy heavy.biteAccuracy Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
0.3 · 0.5 · 0.7 (probability, 0–1)
Allowed bounds
0 – 1 (probability, 0–1)
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Chance that a bite attempt connects. Against a tiger latched onto any T-Rex it is 1.5 times higher (at most 0.95).

Basis. A judgement of how often a bite lands on a small, agile target.

Shake-off rate heavy.shakeRate Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
0.05 · 0.12 · 0.25 1/s
Allowed bounds
0 – 10 1/s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Per-second rate of throwing off each latched tiger. Every 0.1 s a tiger is thrown with probability “rate × 0.1”, so it holds on for 1 ÷ rate seconds on average (4–20 s across the default range). A thrown tiger loses 10% of its max HP.

Basis. There are no skin-thickness data; preserved skin impressions show only small scales [Bell 2017].

Body radius heavy.bodyRadius Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
2 · 2.5 · 3 m
Allowed bounds
0.5 – 8 m
Individual variation
No: set per run only

What it does. Radius of the circle that stands in for a roughly 12 m long animal. It sets the contact distance for latching (radius + 1.3 m), where latched tigers sit (radius + 0.8 m) and the flanking aim point.

Basis. A geometric estimate.

Pack-animal parameters (11)

Body, movement and stamina, latching and attack, and morale values of the animals that attack in numbers. The ranges and sources below are for the default species, an adult tiger (Panthera tigris); the other species are under Species presets.

  • Body mass
  • Sprint speed
  • Cruise speed
  • Sprint endurance
  • Latch endurance
  • Recovery time
  • Latch chance
  • Damage per second
  • Bleed rate
  • Initial morale
  • Morale loss

Body mass pack.mass Literature-based

Default range (min · mode · max)
100 · 180 · 260 kg
Allowed bounds
30 – 500 kg
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Sets the tiger’s maximum HP. A latched tiger slows the T-Rex by its weight, and the damage it takes when thrown (10% of max HP) scales with it.

Basis. Bengal males are about 167–261 kg and females about 116–164 kg (Chitwan data via a secondary source [Wikipedia: Bengal tiger]); wild Amur males average 176 kg [Slaght 2005]. The range covers both sexes.

Sprint speed pack.sprintSpeed Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
11 · 14 · 18 m/s
Allowed bounds
2 – 35 m/s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Within 60 m of its target a tiger sprints at this speed and spends stamina. A routed tiger also sprints while it has stamina left.

Basis. There is no peer-reviewed measurement of tiger sprint speed. Popular figures of 48–65 km/h (13–18 m/s) are unsourced, so the range is set by analogy with measured wild lion locomotion [Wilson 2018].

Cruise speed pack.cruiseSpeed Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
4 · 6 · 8 m/s
Allowed bounds
0.5 – 20 m/s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Speed when farther than 60 m from the target, when falling back to recover, and when routed with no stamina left. It is capped at the same tiger’s sprint speed.

Basis. A judgement for a trot or canter.

Sprint endurance pack.sprintEndurance Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
10 · 20 · 40 s
Allowed bounds
1 – 600 s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. How long a tiger can sprint from full stamina until it is exhausted. When stamina runs out it falls back to recover.

Basis. Tigers are ambush predators that make short charges [Schaller 1967]. The exact time is judgement.

Latch endurance pack.latchEndurance Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
15 · 30 · 60 s
Allowed bounds
1 – 1,200 s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. How long a tiger can hang on. Stamina drains over this time while latched; when it is gone the tiger lets go and recovers. The longer the hold, the more damage per latch.

Basis. A judgement of how long a tiger could hold on to a moving animal 20–95 times its own mass.

Recovery time pack.recoveryTime Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
30 · 60 · 120 s
Allowed bounds
1 – 3,600 s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Time to refill stamina from empty while resting. A tiger re-engages at 80%, and while cruising it recovers at a quarter of this rate.

Basis. A judgement.

Latch chance pack.latchChance Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
0.15 · 0.35 · 0.6 (probability, 0–1)
Allowed bounds
0 – 1 (probability, 0–1)
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Chance that a contact turns into a hold. After a miss the tiger retries 1 s later.

Basis. Tiger hunting success of about 5–50% [Schaller 1967] is only a loose analogue.

Damage per second pack.dps Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
2 · 6 · 12 HP/s
Allowed bounds
0 – 200 HP/s
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Damage per second dealt to the T-Rex while latched. It also adds to the T-Rex’s accumulated wounds, which drive bleeding. In fights to the death (morale off) it is the assumption the result depends on most.

Basis. Tiger bite force is about 1.2–1.5 kN [Wroe 2005; Christiansen & Wroe 2007]. With the T-Rex mapping (4–12 HP/kN) one tiger bite is worth about 5–18 HP; at one bite or claw strike every 1–2 s that is about 2.4–18 HP/s. The default trims the upper tail because the wounds are shallow relative to a T-Rex.

Bleed rate pack.bleed Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
0 · 0.002 · 0.005 1/s
Allowed bounds
0 – 0.1 1/s
Individual variation
No: set per run only

What it does. Makes the T-Rex lose “accumulated wounds × bleed rate” HP every second. A value of 0.002 means 0.2% of the accumulated wound HP is lost per second.

Basis. Tiger canines are about 5–7.5 cm and claws about 8–10 cm [SDZWA]: shallow wounds for an animal of several tonnes, so the range starts at 0 (no bleeding).

Initial morale pack.morale Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
0.4 · 0.6 · 0.85 (0–1 scale)
Allowed bounds
0 – 1 (0–1 scale)
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Each tiger’s starting morale. With the morale model on, a tiger whose morale is below 0.2 on its turn routs. A survived bite costs 0.25 and being shaken off 0.1.

Basis. Tigers avoid injurious fights, and their usual prey weighs about 60–250 kg, occasionally a gaur of about 1 t [Hayward 2012]. An 8 t animal is far outside that range.

Morale loss pack.moraleLoss Speculative

Default range (min · mode · max)
0.03 · 0.08 · 0.15 (0–1 scale)
Allowed bounds
0 – 1 (0–1 scale)
Individual variation
Yes: per-animal N(1, CV) multiplier

What it does. Morale every tiger within 40 m loses when another tiger dies. Applied only with the morale model on. In preliminary runs it was one of the strongest drivers of the T-Rex’s health loss under the defaults.

Basis. A judgement.

Species presets

The simulator covers several species with the same 22 parameters. The large animal that fights alone uses the eleven heavy.* parameters above; the pack animal that attacks in numbers uses the eleven pack.* parameters. Choosing a species makes the ranges below (min · mode · max) the defaults, and the letter next to each value gives its evidence: L literature value, D derived from literature with a stated formula, S speculative, U a popular figure without a peer-reviewed source. Behavioural values (accuracy, morale, latching) are speculative for every species.

The per-parameter notes and hard bounds above describe the default species, T. rex and the tiger. Other species have wider bounds only where they need them (large-animal mass from 100 kg, weapon force from 0.1 kN, pack-animal mass 5–1,000 kg). Only Homo erectus as the large animal goes further, down to human size: mass from 20 kg, attack reach from 0.3 m and body radius from 0.1 m. T. rex and the tiger keep exactly their previous bounds, so older share links still give the same results.

Homo erectus, an extinct human species that lived from about 1.9 million to 110,000 years ago, is modelled on the same terms as every animal: no clothing, tools, weapons, fire or trained fighting skill, only the physical capability of an average adult. It is a thought experiment on physical capability and does not depict or endorse real violence. As the large animal, its attack is bare-hand striking and gripping. A third constant, intimidation, scales the morale a pack animal loses, and its minimum recovery time, when this large animal hits or throws it off: 1 for every earlier species and 0.02 (speculative) for Homo erectus, whose bare hands do little harm.

Two constants are not sampled. hide multiplies the damage a large animal takes from the pack (damage per second and the wounds that drive bleeding) and stands for thick skin, fat or fur (1 for T. rex). radius is the pack animal’s body radius, used for the contact distance, the latched position and the dot size in the replay (0.8 m for the tiger). Sources and calibration notes are in docs/SPECIES.md.

Scenario settings

These are set directly in the scenario panel. Shared links store only the settings that differ from the defaults, and values arriving through a link are clamped to the ranges below.

Large animal heavySpecies

Default
Tyrannosaurus rex
Options
Tyrannosaurus rex, African bush elephant, African buffalo, Brown bear, Woolly mammoth, Homo erectus

The species on the side of the large animals that fight alone. Choosing it sets that species’ ranges and hard bounds for the 11 parameters and its multiplier on damage taken from the pack (a constant for thick skin). Share links store it as heavy.

Pack animal packSpecies

Default
Tiger
Options
Tiger, Lion, Gray wolf, Spotted hyena, Homo erectus

The species on the side that attacks in numbers. Choosing it sets that species’ ranges and hard bounds for the 11 parameters and its body radius constant (used for contact distance and the latched position; 0.8 m for the tiger). Share links store it as pack.

T-Rex count heavyCount

Default
10 animals
Allowed range
1 – 500 animals

Number of adult T-Rex. They start in a vertical line on the left of the arena. Computing time grows with the total number of animals.

Tiger count packCount

Default
100 animals
Allowed range
1 – 5,000 animals

Number of adult tigers. They start scattered at random in a disc on the right; the disc grows with the head count (up to 20% of the arena side).

Monte Carlo runs runs

Default
500 runs
Allowed range
1 – 20,000 runs

How many times the scenario is repeated; more runs narrow the confidence intervals. If runs × (time limit ÷ 0.1 s) × total head count would exceed 5×10¹⁰ agent-steps, the runs are lowered automatically. Changing the run count changes the whole sampling design.

Seed seed

Default
20,261,019
Allowed range
0 – 4,294,967,295

Starting point of every random number. The same seed and settings give identical results in the same browser engine. It is stored in shared links.

Arena side (m) arenaSize

Default
600 m
Allowed range
100 – 5,000 m

Side of the obstacle-free square arena. The groups start the smaller of 200 m and 40% of the side apart. A routed tiger that crosses the edge leaves the battle.

Time limit (s) maxTime

Default
1,200 s
Allowed range
30 – 3,600 s

If neither side has won by then, the battle is a draw. The value is snapped to 0.1 s. Big fights without morale can last several minutes, so a short limit produces more draws.

Individual variation (CV) individualCV

Default
0.08
Allowed range
0 – 0.3

Size of individual variation (coefficient of variation). For every parameter with individual variation, each animal gets a multiplier drawn from a normal distribution with mean 1 and standard deviation CV, truncated to 0.5–1.5. At 0 there are no individual differences.

Sampling sampling

Default
LHS (Latin hypercube)
Options
LHS (Latin hypercube), Simple random

How parameter values are drawn for each run. LHS cuts every range into as many strata as there are runs and samples each once, covering the ranges evenly even with few runs. Simple random draws every value independently.

Pack tactic packTactic

Default
Flank
Options
Flank, Direct charge

How tigers choose and approach a target. Flank spreads the attack across T-Rex and circles behind, avoiding the danger zone in front of the jaws. Direct charge runs straight at the nearest T-Rex. Both are hypothetical.

Morale & routing morale

Default
on
Options
on, off

When on, tigers lose morale when bitten or shaken off and when a tiger within 40 m dies, and they rout below 0.2. When off, tigers fight to the death. It is one of the settings that changes the answer most.

Every match-up is an educational model. T. rex never lived alongside any of the other species, match-ups seen in the wild are heavily simplified, and every interaction rule is an assumption. The model shows how the outcome depends on uncertain inputs; it does not predict a real fight, and this site does not promote or endorse real animal fighting.
